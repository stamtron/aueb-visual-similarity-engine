What can you become?

A Master’s degree is a starting point, not a job title.

  • Researcher? Consultant? ML Engineer? Product Data Scientist? Founder?

02 · THE CAREER LANDSCAPE

Six environments

Different environments reward different strengths.

  • ACADEMIA & RESEARCH · depth, discovery, publications
  • LARGE CONSULTING · breadth, clients, transformation
  • SPECIALIST CONSULTING · hands-on delivery, variety
  • PRODUCT & SCALEUPS · users, metrics, iteration
  • INDUSTRY VERTICALS · domain expertise + data
  • ENTREPRENEURSHIP · ownership, risk, distribution
CAREER ENVIRONMENT ≠ JOB TITLE

03 · ACADEMIA & RESEARCH

Follow the question

From research assistant to PhD and beyond.

  • Typical roles: Research Assistant · Research Engineer · PhD Candidate · Research Scientist · Postdoctoral Researcher
  • Where: universities, research centres, EU-funded projects, industrial R&D.
  • Trade-offs: scientific depth and intellectual freedom versus grant cycles, fixed-term contracts and publication pressure.
  • Greek examples: FORTH / ICS, Athena Research Center, NCSR Demokritos.
BEST FIT: CURIOSITY + PATIENCE + DEPTH
AUEB · MSc Data Science04 / 14
04 · LARGE CONSULTING

Solve problems across industries

Data & AI teams work with clients to change how organisations operate.

  • Typical roles: Data Scientist · Data Engineer · AI/ML Consultant · Analytics Consultant · AI Strategy Consultant
  • Examples in Greece: EY · Accenture · Deloitte · Grant Thornton
  • What you gain: structured learning, client exposure, communication, delivery discipline.
  • Watch out: some projects involve more stakeholder work, integration and presentations than model building.
BEST FIT: VARIETY + STRUCTURE + COMMUNICATION
AUEB · MSc Data Science05 / 14
05 · SPECIALIST CONSULTANCIES

Closer to the implementation

Smaller teams can mean broader responsibility.

  • Typical roles: Applied Data Scientist · ML Engineer · Computer Vision Engineer · AI Consultant
  • You may touch discovery, data preparation, modelling, deployment and client feedback in the same project.
  • Trade-offs: faster learning and ownership, but less formal structure and potentially less support.
  • Ask in interviews: who owns deployment, monitoring, data quality and the client outcome?
BEST FIT: HANDS-ON + VARIETY + OWNERSHIP
AUEB · MSc Data Science06 / 14
06 · PRODUCT COMPANIES

Build something people use

The model is one component of a product.

  • Typical roles: Product Data Scientist · ML Engineer · Applied AI Engineer · Search / Retrieval Engineer
  • Examples: Omilia (conversational AI), Skroutz (commerce), Workable (HR software).
  • Success includes adoption, latency, reliability, user experience, experimentation and business metrics — not just offline accuracy.
  • A product mindset asks: who has the problem, how often, and what changes if we solve it?
BEST FIT: USERS + ITERATION + MEASURABLE IMPACT

07 · GREEK INDUSTRY OPPORTUNITIES

Shipping is a data problem

A strong example of domain-specific product work in Greece.

  • DeepSea Technologies: vessel performance monitoring and optimisation.
  • METIS: maritime intelligence and vessel operations.
  • Signal Ocean: shipping-market data and B2B SaaS insights.
  • Potential problems: fuel prediction, voyage optimisation, anomaly detection, predictive maintenance, computer vision and decision support.
  • Domain knowledge can become a competitive advantage alongside technical skill.

08 · ENTREPRENEURSHIP

Build your own thing

A difficult path — but a real option.

  • Start with a painful problem, not a technology looking for a problem.
  • Validate demand → find a first customer → build a narrow MVP → measure usage → iterate.
  • Beyond engineering: sales, distribution, pricing, finance, hiring and resilience.
  • Possible routes: technical co-founder, bootstrapped SaaS, AI consultancy evolving into a product, research spinout.
THE HARDEST PART IS OFTEN DISTRIBUTION

09 · CHOOSING A DIRECTION

Choose your trade-offs

There is no universally best first job.

  • Do you want depth or breadth?
  • Do you prefer a defined role or ambiguity?
  • Do you want to work on one product or many client problems?
  • Do you enjoy research, implementation, communication, or commercial ownership?
  • Look for manager quality, mentorship, production exposure and learning — not only title.

10 · A REAL AI ORGANISATION

Satalia: algorithms → decisions

An enterprise AI company within WPP.

  • Founded in 2008 as a UCL spinout; acquired by WPP in 2021.
  • Combines machine learning and optimisation to improve business decisions and operational efficiency.
  • Historical product examples: Satalia Delivery (routing and scheduling) and Satalia Workforce (workforce allocation).
  • Teaching point: the value is not “a model”; it is a better decision embedded in an operating process.

11 · WHAT COMES NEXT

Then comes the interview

Most data science hiring processes include a technical case or a take-home.

  • Typical loop: recruiter call → technical screen → take-home or live case → follow-up discussion → team / values.
  • The take-home is not graded on the fanciest model. It is graded on how you clarify, build a baseline, evaluate honestly and explain trade-offs.
  • The second deck walks through a full worked example: a visual similarity engine, built end to end.
  • Code: github.com/stamtron/aueb-visual-similarity-engine

12 · TAKEAWAYS

Your degree opens doors.

What you build, learn and take ownership of determines where they lead.

  • Explore environments, not only job titles.
  • Build evidence: projects, experiments, writing and collaboration.
  • Learn to translate technical work into user and business outcomes.
  • Stay curious. Your first role is a starting point, not a permanent identity.
